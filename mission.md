## THE CASE OF THE MISSING MONA LISA

</div>

> **GCHQ ANALYSTS**
>
> The Mona Lisa has been stolen. Someone connected to the theft has been sending coded messages to the thief. Your job is to uncover what those messages say and follow the clues.

![The Mona Lisa, the painting at the centre of this fictional case](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6a/Mona_Lisa.jpg)

## THE SITUATION

Our investigators have seized a phone belonging to one of the thief's associates. The folder includes a tiny, unfinished Python script from that phone. It is only a starting clue: it does **not** contain a ready-made decoder for the messages we have already intercepted.

That is your challenge: study how the messages were made, figure out how to undo the code, and reveal the clues hidden inside. Each message you crack could bring us closer to finding the thief and recovering the painting.

## YOUR MISSION

1. **Inspect the evidence.** Look at the phone's script and the intercepted messages provided by your teacher. What does the script do so far, and what is still missing?
2. **Spot the pattern.** Work out what happens to a message when it is encoded. Test your ideas on a short example.
3. **Build a decoder.** Create clear instructions, or a small program, that reverses the sending method.
4. **Check your result.** Try your decoder on a message you already know. If the answer looks wrong, debug it and try again.
5. **Report the clues.** Record each decoded message and explain what it tells your team about the thief.

## YOUR ANALYST TOOLKIT

| Skill | How it helps on this case |
| --- | --- |
| **Decomposition** | Break the code and the investigation into smaller, manageable jobs. |
| **Abstraction** | Focus on the parts of each message that matter; ignore distractions. |
| **Algorithmic thinking** | Write the decoding steps in a precise order so they can be repeated. |

## FIELD RULES

- Keep the original intercepted messages unchanged. Work on copies.
- Write down your tests and results so another analyst can check your work.
- A guess is not a decoded message: look for evidence that your method works.
- Share your findings with your team before declaring the case solved.
